# Wall / Rise

A standalone audio visual prototype continuing the Ink / Flight player, set to **“Rewind” by Tanner Royster**. Open this directory through a local HTTP server (for example, a server with HTTP Range support from `public/`, then visit `/rewind/`). The bundled audio is the supplied full song (**167.82 seconds**, displayed as **2:47**). A server that supports HTTP byte-range requests allows efficient seeking without downloading the whole file.

The player streams `assets/rewind-full.m4a` from its native URL. Its attack schedule is precomputed offline in `assets/rewind-full.onsets.json` with the same detector used for local replacement files at 44.1 kHz. The JSON contains a positive `duration` and time-sorted `{time, strength}` events in seconds, with strengths from 0 to 1. If the JSON is missing or invalid, the wall uses its timed rise while playback remains available. Local replacement files are analyzed asynchronously in the browser.

The wall choreography is a provisional timed interpretation: courses accumulate rapidly until you press **Break wall** (or **B**) during playback. Each press starts an 11-second teardown from the current crest to 72% of its height; the wall then resumes building. Pressing again starts a new break at the displayed height. Seeking restores recorded breaks at their original times, while replaying from the end or replacing the audio clears them. A deterministic upper-mid attack estimate adds a row for each detected pluck-like transient in the mix; a steady climb keeps the wall moving between attacks. This does not isolate an instrument. A slow perspective orbit follows the crest and reveals either side of the wireframe wall; reduced-motion mode uses a fixed oblique view. The teardown releases half-speed tumbling blocks and drifting fragments in camera-relative directions. The phase times are creative timing marks, not measured song sections. Edit `WALL_CONFIG` in `wall.mjs` to change the block count, timing, climb, or seed. Replacement audio is analyzed and scales the sequence to its duration. Bass expands the blocks, midrange thickens their lines, and treble raises the copper highlights.

The player includes seeking, volume, fullscreen, a quiet record view, pointer response, and local audio replacement. The Lyrics dialog contains the supplied lyrics with approximate line timing. Cues use seconds from the start of the song, for example `[{"start":2,"end":5,"text":"Your line"}]`.

Press **Rotate view** (or **Y**) to turn the camera across yaw, pitch, and roll. Every press moves to another orientation in 8 seconds, including while audio is paused. Reduced-motion mode changes orientation immediately.

Behind the wall, the original pressure/advection/vorticity fluid solver from [Inkwash](vendor/inkwash-LICENSE.txt), © 2026 Jonathan Whitaker, moves broad pigments across warm paper. This standalone adaptation retains the WebGL2 solver and replaces its paper-grain display with smooth optical absorption in teal, slate blue, and copper. Bass enlarges deposits and flow, mids curl the currents, and treble adds fine wisps. Playback advances the fluid at a fixed 30 Hz; pausing freezes it. Seeking resets and reseeds the fluid at the selected song time, so it resumes with the correct emitter phase without replaying the full preceding simulation. Replaying or replacing audio resets the wash; reduced-motion mode holds a static seed. If WebGL2 or floating-point render targets are unavailable, a soft Canvas wash keeps the wall player usable.

**Background → Sculpture** selects the prior reflective study. This independent WebGL2 renderer projects every view ray onto one continuous, noise-warped interior surface, then shades its folds with broad analytic studio reflections in teal, slate, copper, and cream. There are no separate sculptural objects or particle layer. The background uses a steady playback-time flow with deeper, smoothly evolving warping. Audio attacks do not change its motion or geometry; treble gently lifts the reflected light. Pause holds the exact last frame, seeking derives the field directly from song time, and reduced-motion mode holds a fixed frame. The nested-noise technique was inspired by [Torin Blankensmith’s audio-reactive Shader Park tutorial](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/02/07/audio-reactive-shaders-with-three-js-and-shader-park/); this prototype uses original GLSL and no Shader Park or Three.js dependency. Choose **Ink** to return to the original fluid wash. Ink initializes only when selected or needed as a fallback. Both options use the existing playback render loop; the wall canvas retains its full resolution and frame timing. If the sculpture shader fails, the player selects Ink, then the Canvas wash if Ink is unavailable.

Typography uses a modern sans-serif stack throughout the player and lyric overlays.

## Fractal background

**Fractal** remains an optional background. It ports the published GLSL expression by [Yohei Nishitsuji](https://x.com/YoheiNishitsuji/status/2111673230113272074), whose profile states that code shared on X is MIT licensed. The exact original expression is retained in [`vendor/fractal-original.glsl.txt`](vendor/fractal-original.glsl.txt), with attribution and the license notice in [`vendor/fractal-LICENSE.txt`](vendor/fractal-LICENSE.txt). The port initializes shader variables, guards the singular radius, and supplies an HSV helper; the 99-step field loop, 11 frequency doublings, ray coordinates, and underlying light accumulation remain faithful to the source. A final color grade maps the accumulated light to teal, copper-orange, and silver/cream highlights. It follows playback time without adding audio-driven attacks. Pause freezes the image, seeking reconstructs the selected time, and reduced-motion mode holds its initial frame. **Sculpture** and **Ink** remain available in the Background selector. If Fractal cannot initialize, the player tries Sculpture, then Ink, then the Canvas fallback. Fractal alone uses a capped WebGL framebuffer (350k desktop / 200k mobile pixels); the wall canvas retains full resolution.

## Fold background

**Fold** is the current default trial. It ports [Yohei Nishitsuji’s 18 January 2025 GLSL post](https://x.com/YoheiNishitsuji/status/1880561598982668452); the author’s profile states that code shared on X is MIT licensed. [`vendor/fold-LICENSE.txt`](vendor/fold-LICENSE.txt) records the exact published expression and MIT notice. The WebGL2 port initializes variables, guards the singular radius, and grades only the final accumulated grayscale light in teal, copper, and chrome. It preserves the 119 outer steps, 17 inner doublings, uncentered pixel ray at zero bank, and post-iteration direction rule. The Fold field follows playback time without audio attacks, freezes on pause, reconstructs on seek, and holds its initial frame for reduced motion. Fold and Fractal share one WebGL canvas and one active engine; the selected mode alone draws. A Fold failure falls back through Fractal, Sculpture, Ink, then Canvas. Fold’s background framebuffer is capped at 240k desktop and 140k mobile pixels; the wall canvas stays at full resolution.

While Fold is selected, **Structure** offers **Clouds**, **Caverns**, and **Ribbons**. Clouds retains the original Fold field. Caverns folds angular coordinates into broad chambers with deeper voids; Ribbons stretches and shears the field into long bands. The extra choices transform the mapped field coordinates before the existing accumulation loop, leaving Fold's ray, step counts, timing, color grade, and bank behavior intact. Structure switching redraws the current song time immediately, including while paused. The selection stays chosen when changing backgrounds or replacing audio, and the control appears only while Fold is active.

**Zoom** is a Fold-only projection experiment. **Near · 1×** keeps the original Fold ray, **Wide · 4×** shows four times the field span, and **Far · 10×** is the default on refresh. These settings widen the view into Fold's infinite field; they do not reveal a finite object. The choice redraws immediately while paused and stays selected across background changes and audio replacement. Fold's structure, steps, color, and time continue to behave as before.

Compact **Background** and **Zoom** controls are also available in the player dock, including record view. They stay in sync with the header controls; Zoom appears only while Fold is active.

In **Fold** or **Fractal**, hold **A** or **D**, or press and hold the two curved-arrow buttons, to bank the view left or right by up to 18°. Banking approaches the target slowly (about five seconds to 95%); releasing the control eases back to level at the same pace. Banking rotates the screen rays around the center before each field's original projection; it does not change the wall's Rotate view control. Banking works while audio is paused or the wall is hidden, and remains available in record view. Reduced-motion mode changes the angle immediately while a control is held. Changing backgrounds or replacing audio resets the bank.

In **Fold**, **Fractal**, or **Sculpture**, hold the rewind icon beside the bank buttons to move the background backward at three times real time. Release to let its visuals advance from that point while music plays, or stay still while paused. The control accepts pointer hold or Space/Enter while focused and is disabled in reduced-motion mode. Seeking, replaying, replacing audio, or changing backgrounds returns the visual clock to the song position. The wall, lyrics, satellite, and player timeline continue to follow the actual audio. Sculpture uses the current energy state while rewinding; it does not reconstruct earlier audio energy.

Use the eye icon or **W** to hide/show the wall. Its current structure is preserved while hidden, and wall/debris drawing is skipped. Background playback and lyric controls remain available.

A small Sputnik inspired wireframe satellite is visible by default over every background. Its seamed sphere, inspection hatch, socket hardware, and four tapered antennae rotate once about every 40 seconds of audio, with a gentle tilt. The pose follows playback time, so pause holds it and seeking moves it to the corresponding orientation; reduced motion holds the initial pose. Use the satellite icon or **S** to hide/show it independently of the wall. The satellite is drawn on the existing Canvas layer without another animation loop or dependency.

Bottom player icons select each background and Near/Wide/Far zoom directly. Active choices are highlighted; header dropdowns stay synchronized.

Fold also has individual Clouds, Caverns, and Ribbons buttons in the bottom player, synchronized with the header Structure selector.

Changing backgrounds hides the wall and satellite; their individual icons can show them again. Structure and Zoom changes preserve their visibility.

The versioned `rewind-social-v3.png` sharing cover and production canonical/social metadata are retained; the cover depicts the Fold field with the wall enabled.

### Regenerating bundled onset timing

The timing file was computed from the delivered AAC with `analyzeAudioBlob()` in `onsets.mjs` using browser `OfflineAudioContext` decoding at 44.1 kHz. In a local preview console, import that function, fetch the finalized AAC for this offline preparation step, and save its `{duration, events}` result as `assets/rewind-full.onsets.json`. Record the AAC SHA-256 as `sourceSha256` and `sampleRate: 44100`; regenerate the timing file whenever audio changes. This preparation download is not performed by the player.
