# Wall / Rise

A standalone audio visual prototype continuing the Ink / Flight player, set to **“Rewind” by Tanner Royster**. Open this directory through a local HTTP server (for example, `python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory public` from the repository root, then visit `/rewind/`). The bundled audio is the supplied song sample from **01:00–01:59.54**.

The wall choreography is a provisional timed interpretation: courses accumulate rapidly until you press **Break wall** (or **B**) during playback. Each press starts an 11-second teardown from the current crest to 72% of its height; the wall then resumes building. Pressing again starts a new break at the displayed height. Seeking restores recorded breaks at their original times, while replaying from the end or replacing the audio clears them. A deterministic upper-mid attack estimate adds a row for each detected pluck-like transient in the mix; a steady climb keeps the wall moving between attacks. This does not isolate an instrument. A slow perspective orbit follows the crest and reveals either side of the wireframe wall; reduced-motion mode uses a fixed oblique view. The teardown releases half-speed tumbling blocks and drifting fragments in camera-relative directions. The phase times are creative timing marks, not measured song sections. Edit `WALL_CONFIG` in `wall.mjs` to change the block count, timing, climb, or seed. Replacement audio is analyzed and scales the sequence to its duration. Bass expands the blocks, midrange thickens their lines, and treble raises the copper highlights.

The player includes seeking, volume, fullscreen, a quiet record view, pointer response, and local audio replacement. The wall and lyrics start hidden, and volume starts at zero. Use the eye icon or **W** to show the wall; its current structure is preserved while hidden. The Lyrics dialog contains a provisional audio transcription; bracketed words are uncertain and need review. Cues use seconds relative to the excerpt, for example `[{"start":2,"end":5,"text":"Your line"}]`. Press **Rotate view** (or **Y**) to turn the camera across yaw, pitch, and roll in eight seconds, including while audio is paused. Reduced-motion mode changes orientation immediately.

The **Background** selector offers **Fold** (default), **Fractal**, **Sculpture**, and **Ink**. Fold and Fractal are WebGL2 ports of published GLSL expressions by [Yohei Nishitsuji](https://x.com/YoheiNishitsuji); the exact source expressions and MIT notices are in [`vendor/fold-LICENSE.txt`](vendor/fold-LICENSE.txt), [`vendor/fractal-original.glsl.txt`](vendor/fractal-original.glsl.txt), and [`vendor/fractal-LICENSE.txt`](vendor/fractal-LICENSE.txt). Their final light accumulation is graded in teal, copper, and chrome or silver. Sculpture is an independent continuous reflective shader inspired by [Torin Blankensmith’s Shader Park tutorial](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/02/07/audio-reactive-shaders-with-three-js-and-shader-park/); it uses no Shader Park or Three.js dependency. Ink adapts the WebGL2 pressure/advection/vorticity solver from [Inkwash](vendor/inkwash-LICENSE.txt), © 2026 Jonathan Whitaker, under the MIT license. A soft Canvas wash is the final fallback when WebGL modes cannot run.

Fold, Fractal, and Sculpture use playback time for motion, freeze on pause, and reconstruct the chosen time on seek. They do not use audio attacks to move the field; the wall retains its music response. Ink advances at 30 Hz while playing and reseeds on seek. Reduced-motion mode holds a static field and fixed oblique wall camera. Fold and Fractal share one WebGL canvas, and only the selected engine draws. Background framebuffers have caps independent of the full-resolution wall canvas. Fold and Fractal use many shader iterations and may be demanding on some GPUs; performance across hardware has not been measured.

With Fold selected, **Structure** offers **Clouds**, **Caverns**, and **Ribbons**. Clouds keeps the original Fold field; Caverns folds it into broad chambers, and Ribbons stretches it into long bands. The choice redraws immediately, stays selected across background changes and audio replacement, and appears only for Fold.

With Fold or Fractal selected, hold **A** or **D**, or the curved-arrow buttons, to bank the view up to 18° left or right. The view approaches 95% of the target in about five seconds and eases back at the same rate on release. Banking works while paused or while the wall is hidden; changing backgrounds or replacing audio resets it. Reduced-motion mode changes the angle immediately.

The versioned [`rewind-social-v3.png`](rewind-social-v3.png) share cover is composed from the Fold field at 14 seconds with the wall enabled. The social metadata and canonical URL in `index.html` use the published `/rewind/` path.

**Zoom** is a Fold-only projection experiment. **Near · 1×** keeps the original Fold ray, **Wide · 4×** shows four times the field span, and **Far · 10×** is the default on refresh. These settings widen the view into Fold's infinite field; they do not reveal a finite object. The choice redraws immediately while paused and stays selected across background changes and audio replacement. Fold's structure, steps, color, and time continue to behave as before.

A small Sputnik inspired wireframe satellite is visible by default over every background. Its seamed sphere, inspection hatch, socket hardware, and four tapered antennae rotate once about every 40 seconds of audio, with a gentle tilt. The pose follows playback time, so pause holds it and seeking moves it to the corresponding orientation; reduced motion holds the initial pose. Use the satellite icon or **S** to hide/show it independently of the wall. The satellite is drawn on the existing Canvas layer without another animation loop or dependency.
