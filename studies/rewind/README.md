# Rewind — Wall / Rise concept

The canonical, editable, dependency-free source is [`../../public/rewind/`](../../public/rewind/). [`dist`](dist) points to that published directory so a source edit and the deployed artifact cannot drift. The bundled 59.536-second audio is an excerpt from 01:00–01:59.54 of **“Rewind” by Tanner Royster**, supplied for this prototype. Lyrics in the dialog are a draft transcription; bracketed words are uncertain and should be checked against official lyrics.

From the repository root, run `python3 -m http.server 8765 --directory public` and open `http://localhost:8765/rewind/`. The player needs no build step or remote package. Press Play, then **Break wall** or **B** to release the current wall; **Rotate view** or **Y** changes the camera. Seeking, local audio replacement, volume, record view, fullscreen, pointer response, and editable lyric cues remain available. A slow orbit tracks the wall crest. The build rate follows upper-mid attacks in the full mix, with steady growth between them; it is not an isolated steel-guitar transcription. Break history follows the playback timeline and clears on replay or audio replacement.

The optional **Ink** background adapts the actual WebGL2 pressure/advection/vorticity solver from [Inkwash](../../public/rewind/vendor/inkwash-LICENSE.txt), © 2026 Jonathan Whitaker, under the MIT license. Playback advances the wash at 30 Hz; pause freezes it. Seeking reseeds at the selected time rather than replaying the entire preceding fluid history. A Canvas wash provides a fallback if WebGL2 is unavailable. Reduced-motion mode holds a static field and fixed oblique wall camera.

`rewind-social-v2.png` is a deliberately composed screenshot of the continuous reflective field and rising wall at 14 seconds into this prototype. The static metadata in `index.html` uses the durable `/rewind/` production URL and the versioned cover. Update both when changing the cover.

## Reflective-flow exploration

The player defaults to **Sculpture**, a continuous reflective, nested-noise WebGL2 field inspired by [Torin Blankensmith’s Shader Park tutorial](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2023/02/07/audio-reactive-shaders-with-three-js-and-shader-park/). This uses independent GLSL, not a Shader Park or Three.js dependency. Flow and warping follow playback time without attack-driven movement; the wall retains its music response. Treble subtly affects reflected light. **Background → Ink** restores the prior fluid treatment. Both modes support pause, seeking, replacement audio, reduced motion, and fallback. The sculpture framebuffer is capped independently of the wall canvas.
