# Chapter 7: River Journey

After Castle Island's manifest points upstream, the Quartermaster provisions a repair boat. Skiff, a river pilot and supporting NPC, meets the raid on deck. The crew follows the current toward water that is moving but not reaching the people waiting for it. After 7.10, a short film follows the repair boat upstream toward the water collection station at the second oasis. That station remains a Coming Soon marker.

## Play and progression

River Journey contains ten authored levels, 7.1–7.10, on 9×9 boards. Five existing matching symbols and all five selectable raid leaders carry over. Skiff is in the Raid Party list as an NPC and cannot be selected. Four boat destinations group the levels: helm (7.1–7.3), intake/bow (7.4–7.5), cargo (7.6–7.8), and chart table (7.9–7.10). The boat is a walkable, static perspective scene. The player and Skiff walk or pace on its deck. Skiff's dialogue introduces the rule and offers short contextual tips on return visits.

The route arc now changes the topology as well as the objective. 7.1 keeps the introductory loop. 7.2 reverses it around an island. 7.3 runs across the board and wraps from the right edge to the left. 7.4 has two side-by-side eddies turning in opposite directions. 7.5 freezes only one of those eddies with salt while the other keeps flowing. 7.6 puts cargo in an upper loop; 7.7 uses a winding across-board stream; 7.8 has two side-by-side upper loops entirely above the bottom exit rows. 7.9 staggers two loops at different heights and freezes one. 7.10 combines a winding wrap route with a lower reverse eddy, salt, and crates. These are the existing ten level IDs; saves and replay links remain valid. The exact budgets, masks, routes, goals, and placements are authoritative in `src/game/levels.ts`. `scripts/fixtures/river-winning-routes.json` records a legal no-booster win for every level with every selectable companion. These witness routes prove reachability, not human difficulty.

## Current rule

Blue water tiles and arrows show each path's direction. Symbols in the water swap and match normally. An authored level may have one or two disjoint routes. After an accepted turn's normal clear, fall, and refill, every occupant on every active route moves one cell simultaneously from the same board snapshot. A route may close as an ordinary loop or jump between paired outer edges; the edge jump is shown as a wrap, not as a line crossing the board. The moved board resolves matches before checking crate exits; any resulting clears, falls, and deliveries animate. A carried power does not fire merely because it moved; a power newly created this turn remains protected. Each active route moves only once per accepted turn, including direct power activation. Invalid swaps, hints, reshuffles, and the coin finale do not advance routes.

Any salt crust remaining on a route freezes that route only. A second, unblocked route keeps moving; off-route crust stops neither. The last block may break in the normal clear of a turn; its route then moves in that same turn. Salt broken in the post-current cascade releases the route next turn. A frosted route stays visibly stopped until the salt-break animation completes. Upper loops can carry crates upward, but cargo still exits only when it falls to a valid bottom edge of the board. Every cargo mask retains bottom exits with no hidden internal exit directly under a crate column.

## Playtest tuning

The first complete River export was one experienced Keeper run without starting boosters. Its spare moves for 7.1–7.10 were 16, 5, 6, 10, 10, 12, 8, 10, 7, and 8. This is a useful signal, not a measured win rate. The `chapter-7-3` pass raises ordinary collection targets at 7.1, 7.4, and 7.6, adds an off-route salt seal at 7.5, and narrows the upper banks at 7.8–7.10. Those last three levels gain four, five, and six one-hit crusts respectively, including salt gates below cargo. Level 7.8 begins with the east route frozen and west flowing; 7.9 retains that contrast; 7.10 starts with both routes frozen. All move budgets, starting crates, seeds, and current routes remain unchanged. Levels 7.2, 7.3, and 7.7 stay at their previous targets because that run had less slack there. A new playtest should check whether cargo routing rather than ordinary water cleanup drives the late challenge.
