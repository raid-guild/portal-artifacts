# Raid Guild: The Last Mile

Story proposal — September 19, 2026.

The user endorsed this story direction. Visual planning and generation prompts are in [the art brief](RAID_GUILD_ART_DIRECTION.md); character appearances remain provisional.

Confirmed direction: a tiny point-and-click adventure in the world suggested by Raid Guild's new brand, following a newcomer who finds the Guild and becomes a member. This replaces the earlier provisional science-fiction outpost concept. The characters, locations, and events below are proposed fiction, not established Guild lore or a description of its actual membership process.

## Reading the brand

Reviewed the redesign's page copy and three original illustrations: the desert traveler and floating vessel, the group of working builders, and the floating coral citadel. Also reviewed the linked brand archive's landing-page description. The research browser could not open the redesign; its HTML and original image files were retrieved directly. Interactive site behavior was not tested.

The imagery combines travel, impossible architecture, practical equipment, and human craft. Fine outlines and broad fields of color give the landscapes space. The builders carry tablets and tools, with worn clothes and distinct silhouettes. That suggests a world whose spectacular structures require ordinary maintenance.

The site's community language emphasizes curiosity, shared knowledge, and people making things together. My story interpretation: the protagonist's first connection should come through a small piece of shared work. A specific person needs their help; the relationship continues when they arrive at the Guild.

## Premise

A traveler has crossed the salt flats looking for Raid Guild. They carry a notebook full of unfinished inventions and an old route sketch marked with crossed swords.

At the last waystation, they find a stranded walking machine and its increasingly annoyed pilot. Helping her get home brings them to the Guild—and gives them the beginnings of a place there.

Target: 10–15 minutes, three scenes, four speaking NPCs, three small puzzles,
one ending. There is no combat or time limit. The journey moves from an empty
landscape to a crowded worktable.

## People

**The traveler — player character.** Curious, observant, and accustomed to
working alone. They have followed rumors of a place where someone might help
finish the ideas in their notebook.

**Rook — Guild mechanic and walker pilot.** Returning with workshop supplies. Competent, blunt, and presently lying beneath a machine that refuses to cooperate. She accepts help readily and remembers it. Her name and role are placeholders; this can become a community-created character later.

**Keeper Orin — host of the Guild's common workshop.** Warm, busy, and
interested in the promise behind the work. Orin gives the traveler a raid brief
and keeps the shared need visible.

**Mica — builder.** Careful, practical, and delighted by an imperfect invention.
Mica provides the continuity key and makes the handoff durable.

**Sable — strategist.** A route-maker who connects working things to people and
place. Sable provides the route thread and keeps the path through the world in
view.

## Scene 1: The stranded walker

Coral sand, long blue shadows, an enormous sky. A squat cargo walker leans beside a waystation. Its raised foot has sunk into loose ground. A cloth bearing crossed swords hangs from its side.

Opening text:

> The map ends here.
>
> Someone has written “ask for the Guild” in the margin.
>
> You were hoping for an address.

The traveler calls toward the machine:

> **Traveler:** Is this the road to Raid Guild?
>
> **Rook:** Usually. Today it's where I live.

Rook needs to raise the chassis far enough to reseat a linkage. Her jack sinks when placed directly on the sand. The player examines the equipment, takes a broad metal cargo plate from the walker, puts it beneath the jack, and operates the jack while Rook repairs the mechanism.

Clues are concrete: the jack has left a narrow hole; the plate has a broad flat surface; Rook explains why the lift keeps failing. Clicking the wrong target provides useful feedback. Nothing can be lost or used up incorrectly.

When the walker stands, its cabin light comes on. Rook sees the route sketch and offers a ride to the nearby crossing. She is going home to the Guild.

> **Traveler:** I thought you'd be harder to find.
>
> **Rook:** We move. It complicates directions.

The move to the next scene is a fade with a short walking sound and shifting shadows. No travel simulation is required.

## Scene 2: The crossing

The walker waits beside an old transit arch. Across the gap hangs the Guild's coral citadel. Small vessels drift beneath it. The player can finally see the destination.

The arch serves several destinations, and its route dial still points toward the quarry. Rook is holding the walker steady on the docking contacts and asks the traveler to set the destination.

The route sketch in the notebook identifies the Guild's crest and its two-part transit address. The arch has two matching illustrated rings. The player examines the sketch, rotates the rings to match the address, and pulls the transmit lever. The answer is visible; the puzzle is noticing the connection between an inventory document and the machine. Symbols have text labels as well as shapes.

Wrong settings get a response from the arch and can be changed immediately. They do not create other playable locations. Rook offers a more direct hint if asked.

With the correct address, someone at the Guild answers:

> **Keeper:** Rook? We were about to come looking.
>
> **Rook:** Bring another cup. I've got company.

The arch fills with light. The traveler and walker pass through.

## Scene 3: A place at the table

The Guild's common workshop overlooks the same landscape from above. Maps,
plants, strange instruments, patched furniture, and unfinished work fill the
room. Orin, Mica, and Sable each hold one part of a small raid: context, build,
and route.

The player meets all three roles, then places the raid brief in the open ledger,
the continuity key in the signal frame, and the route thread on the route board.
Wrong pairings give a useful correction and preserve the item. When the three
contributions hold together, the far beacon answers.

Orin turns an open chair toward the traveler. Mica makes room for the notebook,
and Sable adds the new route to the map. The unfinished walking lantern becomes
the hook for **The First Raid**.

The ending gives the traveler a fictional place at the table. It is not a
real-world Guild membership flow.

## What the player does

| Beat | Action | Story consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | Explore the waystation and talk to Rook | Find a person who can help reach the Guild |
| Collaborate | Place the plate, use the jack, let Rook fix the linkage | Earn trust through a shared repair |
| Find the route | Read the sketch, match the transit address, send the signal | Reach the Guild together |
| Assemble | Meet the three roles and connect brief, key, and route | Turn complementary contributions into one raid |
| Join | Take the open chair at the shared table | Earn a place at the table and begin The First Raid |

Portable objects: notebook, cargo plate, jack, raid brief, continuity key, and
route thread. The road tools return to the walker after the repair; the three
Workshop contributions move from inventory to their matching stations.

## Tone and production boundaries

Let the environments carry the wonder. Keep conversation specific, brief, and lightly funny. Reveal the Guild through the way people treat the traveler and their work. The broken walker is an ordinary mishap, not a concealed admission test.

The story requires three backgrounds, one player character, Rook, three original
Workshop roles, a mostly static walker, the transit arch, and a few item icons.
The walker needs only stranded and standing poses; transit can use light and a
fade. These are scene needs, not a description of real Guild people.

There are no real-time deadlines, branching routes, combat systems, mandatory
brand-knowledge quizzes, or technical prerequisites for the player. All three
puzzles are solvable from information inside the game.

The implemented Workshop adaptation, state contract, dialogue, and walkthrough
are recorded in [Chapter three: The Workshop](WORKSHOP.md).
